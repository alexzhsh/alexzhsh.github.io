Blue Sky Solar Racing Design Team

  • THE PROBLEM
  • THE STEPS
  • RESULTS & TAKEAWAYS

  • Solar car could not complete the Figure-8 turning test as prescribed by American Solar Challenge regulations and experienced wheel lift, indicating excessive lateral load transfer
  • Proposed and evaluated solutions such as increasing track width, adding anti-roll bars, and increasing wheel vertical travel limits based on load transfer theory, reliability, cost, and time constraints
  • Identified 60 mm track width increase as most effective solution based on moment balance hand calculations

  • Designed new upper control arm (UCA) by translating existing wireframe points outwards 30 mm while maintaining existing clevises for mounting to the chassis
  • Led all structural simulations for both the new UCA and lower control arm (LCA)
  • Identified errors with force calculator by identifying abnormally high force magnitudes and deriving free body diagrams
  • Analyzed welded steel control arms and transitioned design to machined aluminum due to low predicted safety factors and insufficient time to improve confidence in weld simulation accuracy

  • Designed aluminum 7075-T6 UCA in CATIA, performed FEA across 7 load cases targeting a safety factor of 3 and fatigue lifespan of 1000000 cycles, and iterated upon design with cutouts and fillets
  • Developed best-practices for fatigue modelling, including the use of Goodman mean stress correction theory and fatigue strength factors
  • Conducted FEA on existing upright and clevises to show that they could be retained, saving over $3000 in manufacturing expenses
  • Resolved unrealistically high stress concentrations in LCA simulations by eliminating oversized fillets on bolt hole edges

  • Created engineering drawings for the upper control arm in CATIA
  • Applied GD&T to control features critical to part function and assembly, such as the clevis mounting faces and upright connection position
  • Analyzed tolerance stack-ups and allowable assembly misalignment to determine dimensional tolerance limits

  • Identified CAD model errors and an incorrect interference fit which prevented spherical bearings from fitting properly in the new lower control arm
  • Completely redrafted drawing overnight to correct CAD and implement the proper clearance fit

  • Increased maximum lateral acceleration by 7% based on load transfer calculations
  • Improved figure-8 time by over 2 seconds on average, passing scrutineering and allowing vehicle to proceed to track race
  • Decreased UCA mass by 20% while increasing safety factor by 1.8 to account for lack of fatigue limit in aluminum
  • Sanity checks on structural analysis using hand calculations and free body diagrams are essential
  • Ensure features critical to part function are controlled properly in engineering drawings, including joint locations and bearing holes
  • In future designs, eliminating the fillets/cutouts where the two UCA arms intersect would have further reduced stress concentrations and simplified manufacturing

Blue Sky Solar Racing Design Team

  • THE PROBLEM
  • THE STEPS
  • RESULTS & TAKEAWAYS

  • Sudden failure of the legacy brake caliper mountings during dynamic testing of the redesigned solar car 2 weeks before the American Solar Challenge
  • Diagnosed failure and redesigned components in under 24 hours to ensure vehicle could resume testing

  • Identified failure mode as deformation of brake ear assembly causing clashing against the rotating wheel hub
  • Built new FEA models of brake ears and upright in ANSYS after discovering previous designs were not analyzed properly

  • Revealed a safety factor of 0.7 on the brake ears and over 1.2 mm of combined deformation, confirming failure mode diagnosis
  • Identified out-of-plane bending in thin upright mounting flange due to non-vertical brake caliper mounting

  • Designed initial solution in CATIA based on reinforcing upright mounting flange using spare welded steel while retaining the existing brake ear
  • Since stress was very high on the current brake ears, I believed this design would still experience excessive stress and deformation

  • Hypothesis confirmed via FEA, showing a safety factor of 0.85
  • Designed new mount to completely replace brake ears and connect the calipers directly to the upright, prioritizing minimal risk of structural failure

  • Final welded steel design CAD and FEA

  • New design increased safety factor to 3 and reduced overall assembly deformation by 75%
  • Vehicle successfully completed hard braking tests and passed scrutineering at the American Solar Challenge
  • Deformation is a governing failure mode of the brake caliper mounting assembly and must be considered during structural analysis
  • Position brake caliper in future assemblies to minimize bending stresses
  • Always ensure designs inherited from previous teams are properly verified

University of Toronto Rocketry Design Team

  • THE PROBLEM
  • THE STEPS
  • RESULTS & TAKEAWAYS

  • Analyze and optimize the bulkheads and fuel tanks of UTAT’s liquid propellent rocket
  • Update bulkheads to meet fluid volume requirements, select O-rings, produce engineering drawings, and conduct FEA on final design

  • Leads believed tank was engineered and would fail via bearing failure based on prior FEA
  • Researched bolted joint analysis and performed hand calculations to determine stresses for each failure mode, including bolt tear out, casing tensile failure, bolt shear failure, and bearing failure

  • Contrary to prior belief, calculations showed that the tank was overengineered with a safety factor of 3, and that bolt shear was the actual governing failure mode
  • Validated analysis via hydrostatic burst testing; tank withstood >2.5x design pressure and failed via bolt shear as predicted

  • Proposed and implemented a 33% reduction in tank wall thickness and a 20% decrease in bolt count
  • Redesigned bulkheads with Onshape for updated fluid volumes and produced engineering drawings

  • Selected temperature- and chemical-compatible O-rings
  • Corrected existing groove dimensions and tolerances to match Parker handbook recommendations for proper sealing

  • Conducted FEA with ANSYS on final bulkheads and downcomer tube
  • Calculated worst-case thermal contraction of tank walls to ensure downcomer would not fail through compression, showing a safety factor in excess of 3.5
  • Fuel tank wall thickness reduced by 33% and bolt count by 20%
  • Maintained a safety factor >1.5 on all components for a 1000 psi maximum expected operating pressure
  • Resolved Launch Canada’s concern that downcomer would fail during propellent filling due to tank length thermal contraction, allowing retention of existing design

Multifunctional Composites Manufacturing Laboratory

  • THE PROBLEM
  • THE STEPS
  • RESULTS & TAKEAWAYS

  • Goal: Develop an automated method of evaluating graphene filler dispersion in polypropylene nanocomposites and correlate metrics to electrical conductivity
  • Existing image analysis workflow highly inefficient and required manual inputs of microscopy images
  • Tool could only return degree of blackness and lacked the capability to quantify the formation of co-continuous graphene networks in the composite

  • Developed procedure to heat thin composite samples under pressure to isolate a single layer

  • Selected the DBSCAN clustering algorithm in order to handle to complex data with irregular shapes and noise

  • Developed Python script to separate filler from background, identify clusters, output visualization of graphene networks, and extract quantitative metrics using Scikit-learn, NumPy, OpenCV, Matplotlib
  • Determined that electrical conductivity increases were driven by larger average cluster sizes but a lower total number of clusters due to the formation of co-continuous conductive pathways

  • Reduced analysis time reduced from hours using the existing manual software to seconds with an automated Python script
  • Developed filler dispersion metrics of average network size and total number of distinct networks with a strong correlation to electrical conductivity data; paper currently under review
